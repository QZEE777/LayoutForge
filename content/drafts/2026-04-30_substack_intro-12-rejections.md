# 12 Rejections. What Amazon Never Told Me.

I'm 65.

I live in a country where an aging white guy can't get a job — no matter how ready, willing, or able he is. So last year, after watching enough YouTube videos about making money on Amazon KDP, I decided to give it a real shot.

Supposed to be easy. Everyone said so.

I got a free Canva account. Opened ChatGPT. Bookmarked every influencer video I could find. And I went to work — 12 to 16 hour days for weeks. Building my first book. A crypto-related journal. Funny. Practical. Something I was genuinely proud of.

My mom — 87 years old — said it was wonderful.
My girlfriend said I was amazing.
Friends told me it was great.

I was ready.

---

I uploaded to Amazon KDP.

Day one. Day two. Day three.

Rejected.

The email was vague. Margins. Bleed. TOC. "Your file does not meet our submission guidelines. Please review the KDP Formatting Guidelines and resubmit."

That's it. That's all they give you.

I fixed what I thought was wrong. Re-uploaded. Rejected again.

13 uploads. 12 rejections. Weeks of my life. The same vague emails, the same invisible rules.

I went back to YouTube. Scoured Reddit and Facebook groups. All I found was more confusion — and more people selling courses.

---

Here's what I eventually figured out: the influencers aren't lying, exactly. They just have years of experience that made the technical stuff invisible to them. They forgot what it felt like to not know. And Amazon's rejection system is designed for publishers — not for first-time authors who built their book in Canva at midnight.

The system isn't friendly. It's not clear. And it doesn't warn you.

---

After 5 more books — some mine, some formatted for other people — I'd built up a lot of hard-won knowledge about exactly what KDP checks, exactly where files break, and exactly what the rejection emails are trying to say in plain English.

About 8 months ago, I came across vibe coding. Building software with AI tools. And I thought: I know what the pain points are. I lived them. What if I built something that tells you whether your file will pass before Amazon sees it?

Three months. Over 15 hours a day, every day. Borrowed money for infrastructure.

The result is **manu2print** — a KDP PDF checker that tells you exactly what's wrong with your file before you upload. Margins. Trim size. Bleed. Fonts. The exact issues that caused my 12 rejections, caught before they become yours.
