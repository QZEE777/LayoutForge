Amazon KDP doesn't read your book first.

It reads your file.

Before a single human at Amazon looks at your content, an automated check runs on your PDF. It's looking for three things:

**Margins.**
Your outer edges need at least 0.25 inches of clear space. Your inner margin — the gutter — needs more, depending on how many pages you have. A 300-page book needs a wider gutter than a 100-page one. Most people set it once and forget it.

**Bleed.**
If anything in your design touches the edge of the page — a background colour, an image, anything — KDP expects 0.125 inches of extra space beyond the trim line. Without it, you get a thin white strip on the printed edge. Canva doesn't warn you about this.

**Font embedding.**
Every font in your PDF needs to be baked into the file itself. Some exports skip this step silently. The PDF looks completely normal. KDP still rejects it.

None of these show up when you look at your file on screen.

That's the problem.

Most first-time authors spend weeks on their book and 10 minutes on the file. Then they spend the next two weeks wondering why it keeps getting rejected.
